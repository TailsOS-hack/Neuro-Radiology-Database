# Kevin dementia experiment: Methods protocol snapshot

This draft describes the protocol fixed before held-out model evaluation. Final run completion, selected epochs, measured results and resource cleanup must be reported from their verified output records. It does not assert that every dataset identified in the search was acquired.

## Data sources and study roles

The legacy dataset contained 44,000 two-dimensional images labeled MildDemented, ModerateDemented, NonDemented or VeryMildDemented. These are the supplied image labels; participant identifiers, original augmentation-family identifiers and a clinically verified mapping to CDR stages were unavailable. Upstream augmentation means image counts cannot be interpreted as independent patient counts. The legacy dataset supports an exploratory image classification experiment rather than patient-independent clinical validation. The [current inventory](../datasets/current_local_audit.json) records these boundaries.

Two source releases supplied 98 original T1 MRI volumes and corresponding source participant records: 78 from the [Besga dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3935636), described in its [original data paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7658647/), and 20 from [OpenNeuro ds007561 version 1.0.0](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds007561/versions/1.0.0). Besga comprised 35 AD, 19 control and 24 bipolar records. The 24 bipolar records were excluded from the primary clinical AD/control endpoint. OpenNeuro comprised 1 AD, 14 control and 5 MCI records. Cross-repository participant independence was not established merely from the different repository names. Acquisition provenance and integrity checks are recorded in the [Besga acquisition report](../datasets/besga_acquisition_summary.json) and [OpenNeuro acquisition report](../datasets/openneuro_acquisition_summary.json).

The 54 Besga AD/control participants were assigned to subject-disjoint training, validation and internal test sets using stratified sampling with seed 1729: 32 training participants (21 AD, 11 control), 11 validation participants (7 AD, 4 control) and 11 test participants (7 AD, 4 control). All 20 OpenNeuro participants remained outside model fitting and checkpoint selection. Binary performance on this source therefore has 15 evaluable participants, including only one AD case. The five MCI participants received descriptive predictions and were excluded from binary accuracy, sensitivity and specificity denominators. No mapping of MCI to AD, control or a legacy severity class was introduced.

The ten recommended sources—ADNI, OASIS-1, OASIS-2, OASIS-3, OASIS-4, NACC, AIBL, MIRIAD, NIFD/FTLDNI and ALLFTD—were included in the acquisition inventory, with access and eligibility still pending for their official raw releases. They were not ten additional acquired training cohorts. The [source crosswalk](../datasets/recommended_dataset_crosswalk.json) documents access requirements and overlap concerns, including provider advice against pooling OASIS-1/2/3. Dataset portals and overlapping releases were not counted as independent additive cohorts.

## Image grouping and preprocessing

Legacy images were grouped by connected exact decoded-pixel hashes or identical 64-bit dHashes. Any connected group touching an original100 image was excluded, removing 1,863 rows. Mixed-label hash groups were excluded as a conservative sensitivity rule, removing another 14,959 rows; these exclusions do not establish that the supplied labels were erroneous. The remaining 27,178 images comprised 19,023 training, 2,705 validation and 5,450 test images. Connected groups were confined to a single split. Coarse dHash collisions may connect distinct people, and lack of overlap among hashes does not recover missing participant or augmentation-family identity. The [frozen split audit](../prepared/split_audit.json) records counts, exclusions and manifest digests.

Clinical T1 volumes were converted to canonical RAS orientation, resampled into orthogonal 1.5-mm isotropic space, and normalized per volume using the first and 99th intensity percentiles of a thresholded largest connected cranial foreground. A heuristic detected the cranial extent; nine axial slices were sampled at fixed 45–85% fractions. Cropping used a 6-mm margin; square padding preserved aspect ratio before bilinear resizing to 224 × 224 pixels and grayscale replication into three channels. This produced 666 slices from 74 included source participants. The method did not use validated skull stripping, atlas registration, bias-field correction or scanner harmonization. Details and digests appear in the [preprocessing record](../datasets/clinical_preprocessing_summary.json).

The initial 20–80% slice range was revised after gross anatomical inspection showed excessive inferior tissue. Initial QC sheets displayed source diagnoses, and both clinical sources were inspected before the final rule was frozen; no model outcomes were inspected and no participant was removed on that basis. Thus, OpenNeuro was excluded from fitting and model selection but was not entirely unseen during preprocessing. It is reported as an exploratory external pilot. Gross rendering QC was not radiologist or clinical quality assurance. Age and source sex/gender were retained as descriptive metadata, not model inputs. Besga AD/control mean ages differed, and source appearance and age confounding remain unresolved.

## Model and training protocol

The specified architecture uses a MobileNetV3-Large backbone initialized with torchvision ImageNet-1K V1 weights and a four-class legacy head. The multitask condition adds a separate two-class AD/control head sharing the backbone and penultimate representation. The stage-only condition and multitask condition use the same architecture and stage objective; neither uses initialization from the old study checkpoint. Only the multitask clinical head is specified for clinical training and evaluation. Clinical AD diagnosis and legacy four-stage labels remain distinct endpoints.

Both conditions are specified at seeds 20261011, 20261012 and 20261013, giving six prespecified runs. Each run allows a maximum of 100 epochs, with an early-stopping floor of 20 completed epochs and patience of 15 epochs without a qualifying improvement. Checkpoint replacement requires stage-validation negative log-likelihood to improve by more than 0.0001; consequently, the selected checkpoint need not have the absolute smallest observed loss if subsequent changes are smaller than this minimum delta. Planned reporting retains all runs and their actual completed and selected epochs.

The specified default training pass visits all 19,023 retained stage-training images once per epoch with shuffled batches of 32. Stage loss uses inverse image-group-size and class-group-frequency weights, normalized to mean one. Optimization uses AdamW (initial learning rate 0.0001; weight decay 0.001), label smoothing 0.05, gradient-norm clipping at 5, and cosine learning-rate annealing over 100 epochs to 5% of the initial rate. Training transforms include resizing to 224 × 224 pixels, random horizontal flipping, random rotation within 10 degrees, brightness jitter 0.08, contrast jitter 0.12 and ImageNet normalization. Validation and evaluation omit random augmentation.

In the specified multitask condition, an auxiliary batch is added every fourth stage step with loss weight 0.25. Its 32 draws are balanced across AD and control, with uniform participant sampling within each diagnosis and uniform slice sampling within the chosen participant. The comparison therefore evaluates the full auxiliary-training regimen, including shared representation and batch-normalization updates, rather than isolating only an increase in image count. The evaluation protocol aggregates clinical predictions by averaging the nine slice probabilities within each source participant before validation or test scoring.

The trainer logs raw epoch stage losses as unweighted, unsmoothed cross-entropy, while the optimized stage objective includes weighting and label smoothing. Clinical training losses describe balanced sampled slices; clinical validation losses describe participant-aggregated predictions. Training augmentation and dropout further distinguish training from validation conditions. Planned reporting presents curves unaltered, with these differences disclosed rather than treating a train/validation gap as a directly comparable quantity. The [frozen trainer](../../training/train_candidate.py) and [suite script](../../training/run_training_suite.py) specify implementation details.

## Selection, comparisons and uncertainty

The checkpoint-selection rule uses stage-validation NLL only; clinical validation is recorded but does not select the checkpoint. Before held-out evaluation, the selection rule designates the condition with the lowest mean stage-validation NLL across its three seeds as the validation-preferred condition. It designates that condition's lowest-validation-NLL seed as an exploratory representative, with fixed tie rules. Planned reporting retains both conditions and all six checkpoints. No held-out outcome selects architecture, preprocessing, thresholds, epochs, seeds or conditions.

The prespecified primary legacy comparison uses 1,058 identical image rows assigned to test in both the old strict manifest and new prepared manifest. These rows represent 966 prepared image-similarity groups. Among the primary images, 291 have a prepared-group mate assigned to old training and 302 have a mate assigned to old training or validation; no decoded-pixel match to old train/validation rows was observed within the retained prepared rows. Therefore, this is an exact-row-held-out comparison with asymmetric observed similarity exposure, not a fully independent or equally unseen comparison.

A secondary sensitivity analysis was specified before evaluation and excludes every primary group with an old train/validation mate. It retains 756 images in 733 groups (199 MildDemented, 189 ModerateDemented, 180 NonDemented and 188 VeryMildDemented) and is specified to reuse the frozen primary prediction CSVs without additional inference. Planned reporting includes both full primary and secondary results. This conservative subset changes sample composition and does not establish participant independence. Its [prespecified manifest](../prepared/old_hash_group_unexposed_sensitivity.csv) and [audit](../evidence/sensitivity_audit.json) preserve the original source hashes and excluded groups.

The original100 reader set is a separate historical descriptive benchmark. Its images match 66 old strict training, 9 validation and 25 test rows. The evaluation protocol aligns old and new model predictions and the three fixed readers by case identifier, with recognized labels and missingness audited. These reused image-label comparisons are not independent clinical validation or evidence of superiority over a general population of neuroradiologists.

Planned image-endpoint reporting includes accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro F1, per-class recalls and paired differences. Conditional two-sided 95% percentile intervals use 2,000 deterministic paired bootstrap resamples: whole prepared hash groups for primary and secondary comparisons, and images for original100 because supported participant groups are unavailable. Intervals are exploratory, unadjusted for multiplicity, and conditional on the observed sample and fitted checkpoints; they do not include training or source uncertainty. Degenerate intervals do not establish certainty or equivalence. Planned reporting gives three-seed means, sample standard deviations and ranges separately; three-seed standard deviation is not a confidence interval.

The reporting protocol keeps clinical internal test and external results separate by source and split. Accuracy, AD sensitivity and control specificity use source-participant denominators and two-sided Clopper–Pearson intervals. Exact intervals retain their independence and representativeness assumptions; the single external AD case cannot establish stable sensitivity. MCI probabilities are descriptive only. Complete prespecified rules, missingness handling and source references appear in the [analysis usage note](../DATA_AND_REPRODUCIBILITY.md) and [frozen statistics program](../../analysis/summarize_frozen_evaluation.py).
