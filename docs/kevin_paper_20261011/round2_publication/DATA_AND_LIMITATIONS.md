# Data provenance, access and limitations — manuscript draft

This document accompanies [Methods](Methods.md) and [Results](RESULTS.md). All six Round 2 training runs, selection, frozen evaluations and figure review are complete. The new clinical ensemble classified every fresh Harvard case as AD, including all 10 controls, and scored 9/19 versus the old ensemble's 10/19. Clinical readiness and superiority were not demonstrated. Attribution replay exceeded its frozen probability tolerance in two of 100 cases despite unchanged classes, so those figures remain explicitly flagged diagnostics. Public source availability, a respected repository or a good-looking learning curve does not by itself establish valid labels, independent patients or clinical utility.

These counts must not be added together as unique people. Besga/OpenNeuro yielded 666 prepared slices from 74 included source participants; Harvard yielded another 261 slices from 29 available T1 volumes. Multiple slices remain within a single patient unit. Different repository names and absence of exact duplicate files do not prove that all underlying people are independent of each other or of the unidentified legacy source.

Recorded source terms are CC BY 4.0 for Besga and AlzaSet, CC0 for the pinned OpenNeuro release, and CC0 1.0 for Harvard Dataverse v2. The legacy chain remains unresolved: local provenance records Apache 2.0 for the augmented derivative, LGPL 3.0 for its declared upstream, and incomplete original-source compatibility evidence. This is a report of saved provider metadata, not a new legal determination. Newly acquired clinical MRI, subject tables, raw headers and record-level outputs remain outside the public derivative. Provider attribution and actual release terms still govern any future redistribution.

## Public alternatives when controlled-source access is unavailable

The author-released Besga, OpenNeuro and Harvard datasets provide reproducible source records and structural images without substituting anonymous image mirrors for original MRI provenance. Harvard was obtained through the provider’s ordinary public interface, and its archive and per-file checksums matched provider metadata. The original study reports 30 participants and includes anatomical T1 acquisition, but only 29 structural volumes were released. One AD T1 is unavailable. All 29 available T1 volumes passed the unchanged preparation and gross quality checks, producing 261 complete slices with no exclusions. The binary denominator is therefore 19, not 20, 29 or 30; MCI remains outside binary scoring.

The ten earlier recommended official sources—ADNI, OASIS-1/2/3/4, NACC, AIBL, MIRIAD, NIFD/FTLDNI and ALLFTD—are access candidates, not ten newly acquired training cohorts. No approved access or new use of those official raw releases is asserted. Source-specific applications, agreements, permitted-use conditions and cohort overlap must be checked before inclusion. Official entry points include [ADNI data access](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/data-samples/adni-data/), [OASIS access](https://sites.wustl.edu/oasisbrains/home/access/), [NACC requests](https://www.naccdata.org/data-request-process/) and [AIBL collaboration](https://aibl.org.au/collaboration/). NCBI/PubMed and PubMed Central support literature discovery and article access; locating a paper there does not confer access to every underlying image collection. The release linked by each paper is the acquisition authority.

AlzaSet illustrates why public availability is insufficient for inclusion. Its article reports 12,947 images from 79 patients, whereas the verified archive contains 12,741 JPEGs and no patient mapping or clinical table. Two exact image-duplicate groups cross provider validation/test folders. Filename tokens do not establish patient identity or validate the nominal provider split. Therefore, no 79-patient cohort, independent external test or new training set is claimed. The recorded provider publication date is 2027-01-01 despite publicly served files and an inactive embargo at acquisition; the original article described an embargo. That discrepancy is retained rather than reinterpreted as either an access grant or evidence of patient independence. No restriction was bypassed. Only integrity and duplicate checks were performed. Reconsideration requires provider-backed patient/series mapping, label/sequence verification and resolution of the inventory discrepancy before any model use.

## Legacy overlap and the retained reader benchmark

The four labels of the augmented legacy source are not established clinical stages. Exact decoded-pixel or identical-dHash grouping can conservatively identify related images, but collisions can also connect different images, while unrecognized augmentations or shared patients can remain. Patient identifiers, original acquisition cohorts and original augmentation families are missing. The retained 27,178-image split cannot be described as patient-independent.

The historical primary comparison contains 1,058 rows that are individually assigned to test in both manifests, representing 966 prepared similarity groups. Of these rows, 291 have a prepared-group mate in the old training split and 302 have a mate in old training or validation. Thus “exact-row held out” is defensible; “equally unseen by both models” is not. The 756-image sensitivity subset, spanning 733 prepared groups, removes those observed old-development group mates. Its class composition is 199 MildDemented, 189 ModerateDemented, 180 NonDemented and 188 VeryMildDemented. It changes sample composition and cannot repair absent patient identity or every unobserved relationship. Both sets remain visible, with no replacement of the larger comparison by a favorable subset.

The original100 comparison is retained to answer the historical model/reader question on exactly the same cases. Its images match 66 old training, 9 old validation and 25 old test rows. All three fixed reader records and all specified model predictions must align on the 100 images. The reader mean and min–max range are descriptive across those three readers; the range is not a 95% confidence interval. This dataset cannot establish independent clinical performance or superiority to neuroradiologists generally. It uses a different endpoint from the binary T1 patient experiment and must not be used to score that clinical model.

All legacy test cohorts, including the 5,450-image full split, had already been inspected before Round 2 development. Fixed validation-only checkpoint selection limits further outcome-driven choices, but does not restore those cohorts to untouched external tests.

## Clinical transportability and uncertainty

Clinical labels are provider AD/control groups, not new adjudications by this team. The clinical model develops on 32 training and 11 validation patients from one source. The fresh binary external sample contains only 9 AD and 10 CN patients. These are convenience case-control cohorts, not a prospective clinical workflow or a representative prevalence sample. Source scanner, protocol, age and other demographic differences can confound image classification. For example, the Besga descriptor reports 1.5-T imaging, while the original Harvard study used a 3-T Siemens system and anatomical MDEFT. Published demographic summaries for the full original study must not be substituted for summaries of the actually available T1 subset.

The uniform nine-slice heuristic is not a full three-dimensional assessment. It supplies no validated brain segmentation, anatomical registration, scanner harmonization or quality-rejection system. Gross visual QC assesses rendering and gross anatomy; it does not establish diagnostic adequacy or pathology localization. Exact raw, canonical-volume and prepared-pixel audits found no Harvard matches to the acquired Besga/OpenNeuro data, but cannot prove global patient independence from every legacy or unreleased source. The new source remains untouched by fitting, checkpoint selection, threshold selection or recalibration.

Patient-level exact intervals and paired conditional bootstrap intervals must accompany point estimates. OpenNeuro has only one AD participant, so its apparent accuracy can be dominated by controls and its sensitivity precision is inherently weak. A diagnosis-stratified bootstrap repeatedly resamples the same AD person; a narrow conditional sensitivity interval is not population precision. Three training seeds describe optimization variation and do not multiply the patient count or measure uncertainty from alternative development partitions. Sparse reliability bins, softmax scores, Brier scores and case-control predictive values do not establish calibrated clinical risk. No calibration fit, threshold search or abstention rule is introduced in this experiment.

MCI predictions are descriptive research scores. They are not evidence of early AD detection, diagnostic accuracy, progression prediction or a conversion rate. The new clinical ensemble and earlier clinical ensemble differ in optimization, task formulation and slice aggregation; the observed differences are whole-pipeline comparisons, not causal evidence that a single component caused improvement.

## Robustness, explanations and reporting boundaries

The fixed brightness, contrast, noise, blur, compression, resolution and masking controls measure sensitivity to specified image changes. They do not reproduce motion physics, scanner-domain shifts, every acquisition failure or clinical workflow variation. Preserving performance under some controls does not establish general robustness, and performance losses under artificial central occlusion are not themselves a clinical endpoint.

Native 7 × 7 Grad-CAM cells have broad overlapping receptive fields. A colored corner in an interpolated overlay is not proof of dependence on input-corner pixels. Unchanged native maps, fixed erasure/region-only controls, probability changes and parameter randomization are reported together. No heatmap is shifted, cropped or visually optimized to appear more anatomical. A positive mechanics check or absence of observed corner dependence does not prove absence of all shortcuts, faithful localization or biological validity. No model is chosen because its heatmap looks more central.

The attribution-to-evaluation probability replay check remained blocked: two maximum absolute probability differences, approximately 0.0001177192 and 0.0003736615, exceeded the prespecified 0.0001 tolerance. Class predictions agreed. No tolerance widening or model rerun was used to clear this failure. A final-metadata serialization error was recovered separately from saved arrays and tables, with original files preserved. Warning-bearing public derivatives retain the blocked marker and original numerical values. Visual-review completion addresses presentation quality and does not establish replay agreement or anatomical validity.

The full fixed model roster, all scenarios, both fresh/reused clinical distinctions, raw unsmoothed curves and missing/failed checks remain visible. Better training loss alone is not better generalization. Clinical training completed 80/53/78 epochs and selected epochs 65/38/63 using validation only. Its external outcomes expose a major transportability failure: all 19 Harvard predictions were AD, while the new ensemble predicted CN for all 15 reused OpenNeuro participants and missed the sole AD case. The latter's 14/15 accuracy is therefore not evidence of reliable AD detection. All results and figures were verified from retrieved outputs. Institutional ethics/secondary-data oversight and journal-specific access language still require the authors’ actual determination; this document does not invent an approval or exemption.

A further study needs additional verified patient-labeled AD/CN training cohorts and a fresh untouched external cohort, with patient- and source-separated validation. Harvard is now an observed evaluation cohort and cannot become fresh again. No parameter or threshold adjustment based on its outcomes is part of this completed experiment. Healthy-only collections and unmapped two-dimensional mirrors cannot fill the paired AD/CN, multisite evidence gap. More epochs on the same small source are not a substitute for this evidence. Any later calibration, abstention or quality-rejection component must be developed with separate development data and evaluated under a newly frozen protocol.
