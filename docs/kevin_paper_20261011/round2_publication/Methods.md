# Methods — manuscript draft

**Status:** All six Round 2 training runs, validation-only selection, frozen stage/clinical evaluation, verified retrieval and figure review are complete. The fresh clinical result did not demonstrate readiness: the new ensemble classified all 19 Harvard participants as AD, including all 10 controls. See [Results](RESULTS.md) for all denominators and uncertainty. Attribution is retained as a separately labeled diagnostic appendix because two of 100 replayed probability vectors exceeded the frozen tolerance, with unchanged predicted classes.

## Study design and endpoints

We conducted an exploratory four-class image-classification experiment and a subsequent validation-guided robustness development round. The supplied labels were MildDemented, ModerateDemented, NonDemented and VeryMildDemented. These labels were retained without asserting equivalence to clinically adjudicated dementia severity or Clinical Dementia Rating stages. The original reader comparison was retained as a reused image benchmark. A separate, prospectively specified analysis examined provider-labeled Alzheimer’s disease (AD) versus control (CN) using structural T1 MRI and patient-level predictions. No mapping between the binary clinical endpoint and the legacy four-class labels was introduced.

Earlier legacy benchmark results were known before Round 2 was designed. Its repeated evaluations are therefore post-hoc development comparisons. Within Round 2, checkpoint selection used only the fixed training/validation procedures, and all three selected models were frozen before repeated benchmark inference. The fresh Harvard clinical cohort was prepared without model predictions and used once after the separate clinical experiment was frozen. It did not enter fitting, checkpoint selection, threshold adjustment or calibration.

## Data sources and denominators

The legacy source contained 44,000 upstream-augmented two-dimensional images from the [Singhal Kaggle derivative](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/aryansinghal10/alzheimers-multiclass-dataset-equal-and-augmented). Verified patient identifiers and original augmentation-family identifiers were unavailable. Connected components were formed using identical decoded-pixel hashes or identical 64-bit difference hashes. All components touching the original 100-image benchmark were excluded, removing 1,863 rows. Mixed-label components were also excluded, removing 14,959 rows. The latter is a conservative grouping rule, not proof that every excluded label was incorrect. Remaining components were confined to one split, with conflicting assignments moved toward the protected validation/test split rather than into training. This produced 27,178 images: 19,023 training, 2,705 validation and 5,450 test. Image hashes do not establish patient identity.

Clinical MRI was obtained from the [Besga Zenodo release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3935636), [OpenNeuro ds007561 v1.0.0](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds007561/versions/1.0.0), and [Harvard Dataverse DOI 10.7910/DVN/29352, version 2](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/29352). The Besga source is documented by its [primary data descriptor](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2020.583212). Harvard’s dataset title refers to resting-state fMRI, but the release also supplies anatomical T1 MDEFT scans described in the [original study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0120988). Only structural T1 images enter the present clinical pipeline.

## Image preprocessing

Legacy images retained their full available two-dimensional field of view and were resized to 224 × 224 pixels, converted to RGB, scaled to the unit interval and normalized with ImageNet channel statistics. Their prior acquisition, processing and augmentation histories are not recoverable from the image count.

For clinical T1 data, a single unchanged preprocessing implementation converted volumes to canonical RAS orientation and resampled them onto an orthogonal 1.5-mm isotropic grid using linear interpolation. A foreground heuristic retained the largest connected component above 8% of the 98th percentile of positive intensities. The longest axial run with foreground area at least 35% of its maximum defined a cranial span. Nine distinct axial slices were sampled at fixed fractions 0.45, 0.50, 0.55, 0.60, 0.65, 0.70, 0.75, 0.80 and 0.85 of that span. Intensities were clipped and scaled using per-volume foreground percentiles 1 and 99. A fixed in-plane foreground crop with a 6-mm margin, aspect-preserving square padding and bilinear resizing produced 224 × 224 grayscale images replicated across RGB channels.

The clinical procedure is a heuristic representation, not validated skull stripping, atlas registration, bias-field correction or scanner harmonization. Gross image review is not radiologist adjudication. Besga and OpenNeuro had been inspected when the historical slice rule was developed; their preprocessing evaluation was therefore exploratory. Harvard used the unchanged rule, with all 29 complete nine-slice sequences reviewed without model outcomes. No external-cohort normalization parameters, thresholds or calibration functions were fitted. Nine slices constitute one patient bag, not nine independent patients.

## Completed stage-model development

The earlier experiment trained ImageNet-initialized MobileNetV3-Large models under stage-only and multitask conditions at seeds 20261011, 20261012 and 20261013. The multitask condition used a separate AD/CN head while retaining the four-class stage head. All six results remain in the historical record, with their completed run details preserved in the [earlier Methods addendum](../publication/reports/Completed_Methods.md). Round 2 continued the three corresponding validation-selected stage-only checkpoints; it did not train a clinical head.

Each Round 2 epoch visited all 19,023 training images once. The full network was optimized with AdamW, batch size 64, initial learning rate 3 × 10⁻⁵, weight decay 0.001, label smoothing 0.05 and gradient-norm clipping at 5. Loss weights retained inverse image-similarity-group size and class-group balancing. A fixed cosine schedule approached 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ at 100 epochs. The maximum was 100 epochs, with no early stopping before epoch 20 and patience of 15 consecutive epochs without an eligible score improvement exceeding 0.0001. The earliest checkpoint was retained on ties, and the unmodified initialization was an explicit eligible epoch-0 fallback.

Training augmentations comprised horizontal flips with probability 0.5; intensity factors sampled from 0.9–1.1; Gaussian noise with probability 0.5 and standard deviation sampled from 0–0.02 on the unit scale; Gaussian blur with probability 0.25 and radius sampled from 0–1 pixel; and restricted background changes with probability 0.5. Background replacement used constant or low-frequency texture with amplitude up to 0.12, applied either globally or within an 8-pixel border plus 16-pixel corners. All background changes were restricted to the complement of a protected support mask: grayscale values above 8/255, filled internal holes and five pixels of dilation. Images with less than 1% detected support were fully protected. Protected RGB pixels were checked for exact equality after background edits. This mask is not brain segmentation; intensity, blur and noise transforms could affect foreground tissue. No central cropping or unrestricted corner erasure was introduced during training.

Full clean validation used all 2,705 images. A fixed subset of 256 validation images contained 64 distinct prepared hash groups per class, selected deterministically. Eight fixed perturbations formed four equally weighted families: two background conditions, intensity ×0.9/×1.1, noise standard deviation 0.01/0.02, and blur radius 0.5/1.0. The checkpoint score was one-half clean validation NLL plus one-half the mean of the four stress-family NLLs. Eligibility required clean accuracy and balanced accuracy to remain within 0.002 of the same-device initialization and clean NLL to be at most 1.2 times initialization NLL plus 0.002. These are experimental selection guards, not clinical acceptance criteria.

All three runs ended by the specified validation plateau rule. No run reached the epoch ceiling. Completed and selected epochs were:

|---|---:|---:|
| 20261011 | 21 | 6 |

The validation-only representative was seed 20261011, chosen by the smallest eligible selected score with ascending seed as the tie rule. It is an attribution representative; all three models remain in the quantitative comparison. Stage evaluation outcomes did not determine this designation.

## Completed frozen stage evaluation, robustness and diagnostic attribution

The fixed comparison includes the unchanged original model, the earlier stage-only representative c3, and all three Round 2 models. Four cohorts remain separate: the full 5,450-image prepared test split; the 1,058 exact-row intersection of old and new test manifests; its 756-image conservative sensitivity subset; and the original 100-image reader set. All are previously seen development benchmarks in Round 2. The 1,058 and 756 predictions are exact subsets of the full 5,450-image pass.

Thirteen unchanged diagnostic conditions were evaluated for all five models on the 1,058- and 100-image cohorts: clean; brightness ×0.9/×1.1; contrast ×0.9/×1.1; Gaussian noise standard deviation 0.01/0.02; blur radius 0.5/1.0; downsampling to 192 or 160 pixels followed by resizing; and JPEG quality 90 or 70. Noise realizations and inputs were shared across models. Accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro F1, NLL, multiclass Brier score, label flips and probability total variation were retained. These are two-dimensional pixel diagnostics, not physical simulations of MRI acquisition.

A fixed 160-image validation sample, stratified as 40 images per class and one per hash group, supports clean and 12 masking controls. Controls erase or retain only four 32 × 32 corners or a 16-pixel border, using black and ImageNet-mean fill; matched central erasures remove equal pixel areas. This sample is a development diagnostic, with known historical exposure by the old model. It does not prove absence of every shortcut.

Grad-CAM retained all 115 previously fixed case slots: the original 100, eight historical class-balanced examples and seven earlier gallery examples. The original model’s and c3’s 230 verified native arrays remained unchanged. New maps used the validation-selected representative, targeted each model’s own unmodified predicted class and labeled differing class targets. Native 7 × 7 maps supported corner, outer-ring and central mass summaries; interpolated overlays were display products only. Scores are not anatomical probabilities. The original CUDA run checked model state, gradients, modes and logits, and three fixed randomization seeds tested head, head-plus-final-block and full-network randomization on the eight examples. All 115 new native maps were valid; all 72 randomization records were retained, including any recorded flat or failed maps. [Grad-CAM original method](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2017.74).

The final attribution metadata write failed on a NumPy-integer serialization error after saved arrays, tables and figures had been produced. A separate postprocessing recovery preserved the failed output and independently recomputed saved-array mass, randomization and probability-replay checks without rerunning a model. The original runtime state assertions are documented as original control-flow evidence, not assertions rerun during recovery. Two original-100 cases exceeded the maximum absolute probability-difference tolerance of 0.0001: approximately 0.0001177192 and 0.0003736615. Both retained their class predictions. The tolerance was unchanged and the blocked replay marker was preserved. Every public attribution panel and both summary figures therefore carry a diagnostic warning; their visual review does not convert the replay failure into a pass. These outputs are not evidence of anatomical localization or clinical validity.

## Completed clinical training and prespecified evaluation

The clinical model used ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Large features, global pooling to 960 features, dropout 0.2 and a two-logit linear head. One patient prediction is the softmax of the mean of that patient’s nine slice logits. Each of the 32 training patients contributed one unweighted cross-entropy term per epoch. All BatchNorm running statistics and affine parameters remained frozen. Only the head trained for five epochs; subsequently the final three feature blocks and head were trainable.

Three seeds, 20261021–20261023, used patient batch size 4, AdamW learning rates 10⁻⁴ for the head and 10⁻⁵ for the trainable backbone, weight decay 0.001 and gradient clipping at 5. The fixed cosine schedule extended to 100 epochs. Selection used unweighted clean NLL on the same 11 Besga validation patients, with epoch-0 fallback, minimum improvement 0.0001, minimum 20 epochs and patience 15. All three runs passed the prescribed convergence checks and stopped on validation plateau, rather than a safety or epoch ceiling.

These are validation-selection statistics, not external-test results. The complete original training archive, including the three optimizer-bearing final checkpoints, was retrieved and verified. Later evaluation migration retained byte-identical selected checkpoints and histories; it did not retrain or resume the models.

Patient-consistent augmentation uses expanded-canvas rotations within ±5°, brightness 0.92–1.08, contrast 0.9–1.1, optional noise up to 0.01 and blur up to 0.5 pixel. Noise realizations vary by slice while the bag shares transform parameters. No flips, translations, cutout or additional anatomical crops are introduced.

The primary new model is the fixed uniform mean of all three validation-selected patient probability vectors. Its primary comparator is the uniform mean of the three unchanged earlier clinical heads c2/c4/c6. Those heads preserve historical mean-slice-probability aggregation, whereas the new system uses mean-slice-logit aggregation; this is a complete-pipeline comparison, not an isolated pooling ablation. All six individuals and both ensembles were reported. AD is positive, with a fixed probability threshold of 0.5; no fitted calibration or threshold search was performed. The three new seeds are not statistically paired to the three old seeds. Pairing is by patient.

Harvard 19-patient results were reported separately from reused Besga 11 and OpenNeuro 15. Fifteen MCI participants remained prediction-only. Seven fixed conditions comprised clean, intensity ×0.9/×1.1, noise standard deviation 0.01/0.02, and blur radius 0.5/1.0. All conditions were applied to every individual model before both ensembles were reconstructed. The complete grid contained 3,360 patient/model/scenario records and 168 cohort-level metric records. The reporting pass independently reconstructed both ensembles and recomputed every metric from saved predictions without additional inference.

## Statistical analysis and figures

Stage reporting uses accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro F1, class confusion counts, one-vs-rest ROC and precision–recall, NLL, multiclass Brier score and ten-bin top-label ECE. Each Round 2 seed is compared with both fixed comparators using 2,000 deterministic paired bootstrap draws. Prepared hash groups are resampled together for supported legacy cohorts; original100 uses image-level resampling. These conditional, exploratory intervals do not establish patient-level uncertainty or account for source sampling and training uncertainty. All seed values and mean/sample standard deviation are retained. Multiple comparisons are not promoted into confirmatory superiority claims.

Clinical reporting uses patient-level accuracy, AD sensitivity, CN specificity, balanced accuracy, macro F1, ROC AUC, average precision, NLL, binary Brier score and five fixed AD-probability reliability bins. Accuracy, sensitivity and specificity use two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson intervals. New-ensemble and individual-new-model differences from the old ensemble use 10,000 deterministic paired patient bootstrap draws, stratified by observed diagnosis counts, plus exact McNemar discordance diagnostics. The same patients are resampled for both compared models. Intervals are conditional and unadjusted exploratory summaries. In particular, repeatedly resampling OpenNeuro’s sole AD participant cannot estimate population sensitivity precision; its exact interval and denominator remain visible.

Raw training and validation curves remain unsmoothed. Online augmented-training NLL, weighted/smoothed optimization loss, clean-training resubstitution loss, clean-validation loss and stress selection score retain distinct definitions. Stored clean patient NLL in the clinical trainer uses its fixed 10⁻¹⁵ clipping and held-out reporting uses the fixed statistics helper’s 10⁻¹² clipping. No curve is altered to suggest better convergence.

Publication figures cover all-seed learning curves, cohort-specific confusion/ROC/PR, confidence distributions, reliability/calibration diagnostics, seed spread, reader/model comparisons, every fixed stress scenario, native attribution summaries and all 115 case panels. The 28 stage quantitative/robustness graphs, two warning-bearing attribution summaries, all 115 diagnostic panels, 19 clinical graphs and six raw training-curve pairs were rendered and visually reviewed. Exact reviewed figure hashes accompany the public export. The three fixed readers are aligned on all 100 legacy cases. Their mean and min–max range describe those readers and are not confidence intervals or evidence about a population of neuroradiologists.

## Hardware and execution roles

Stage and clinical training used an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPU. The recorded training environment used PyTorch 2.8.0 with CUDA 12.8; the stage record identifies Python 3.12.3. Stage benchmark inference and the original attribution run used CUDA on that training machine. Diagnostic serialization recovery, statistical reporting and visual packaging used saved outputs and did not perform new model inference. The final frozen clinical comparison ran on NVIDIA B300 SXM6 AC after verified migration of the completed clinical models. The B300 pre-evaluation readback confirmed PyTorch 2.8.0+cu128, torchvision 0.23.0+cu128 and CUDA runtime 12.8. B300 was used for evaluation only, without retraining these selected models. All outputs and original training states were verified locally before the project's GPU and storage resources were deleted.

## Reproducibility and publication status

Protocols, manifests, input hashes, code hashes and selected checkpoints are frozen before their corresponding inference stage. Evaluation requires complete model/cohort/scenario membership and unchanged identities; reports independently reconstruct fixed ensembles and recompute metrics from aligned stored probabilities. Retrieval requires archive and individual-payload verification plus local destination readback. Public derivatives contain aggregate results, reproducible code and figures, excluding private patient identifiers, raw clinical image paths, headers and cloud resource identifiers. Exact patient independence, clinical readiness, superiority and the absence of all shortcuts are not established by these technical checks.

Before final submission, add the institution’s actual secondary-data ethics/oversight determination and confirm journal-specific data-access and licensing language. No new ethics approval or exemption is asserted here. The completed research workflow does not resolve the clinical generalization failure or the need for additional independently labeled training and external evaluation cohorts.
