# Independent scope review of the separate clinical pilot

This experiment evaluates provider AD versus control labels from structural T1 MRI. It is separate from the four-stage legacy image benchmark and its neuroradiologist comparison. The clinical training protocol specifies 32 Besga training patients, 11 Besga validation patients, and three fixed seeds. Validation alone selects the checkpoint for each seed; the primary system is the fixed equal-probability ensemble. These are protocol statements, not claims that training or external evaluation has finished.

The acquired [Harvard Dataverse dataset](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/29352) supplies 29 usable released structural volumes: 9 AD, 10 controls, and 10 MCI. The publication describes 30 participants, but one AD participant has no released T1 volume. The prespecified fresh binary test therefore has **19 patients**, not 20 or 30. The 10 Harvard MCI participants receive prediction-only outputs. The acquired and prepared counts, source diagnosis join, fixed nine-slice bags, and gross visual QC are recorded in the [Harvard provenance folder](../round2_external_data/dataverse_29352/README.md).

The other clinical cohorts remain separate: Besga test has 11 previously evaluated patients (7 AD, 4 controls); OpenNeuro has 15 previously evaluated binary patients (1 AD, 14 controls), plus 5 MCI prediction-only participants. There are 60 patient bags and 540 slices in the evaluation manifest, but only 45 binary-evaluable patients across three distinct cohorts. Pooling these cohorts into a single headline score would hide source composition and reuse. Nine slices and three seeds do not increase the patient sample size.

The comparison preserves three old clinical heads individually, their fixed ensemble, the three new seeds individually, and their fixed ensemble. The old system pools slice probabilities; the new system applies softmax to the mean slice logits. This is a comparison of complete training and pooling pipelines, not an isolated test of architecture or pooling. All systems must be evaluated on exactly the same patients for each contrast. The evaluation and report guard tests use synthetic images and small synthetic models only; passing them provides evidence about implementation, not medical performance.

The fresh Harvard cohort can supply a small source-heldout transportability observation after the frozen evaluation. It cannot establish population-level sensitivity, clinical readiness, risk calibration, or prospective utility. Exact raw, canonical-voxel, and prepared-pixel overlap checks found no duplicates against the existing acquired cohorts; these checks do not establish globally unique patient identities across every public dataset. Gross anatomical QC is neither clinician adjudication nor a validated segmentation/registration audit.

All performance intervals remain patient-level. Exact binomial intervals accompany accuracy, AD sensitivity, and control specificity. The paired bootstrap conditions on the observed diagnosis counts. In particular, resampling OpenNeuro's sole AD patient cannot estimate between-patient variability for AD sensitivity; its exact binomial interval and denominator must stay visible. Seed variability is optimization variability, not additional patient uncertainty. Unadjusted paired comparisons are exploratory and do not support a clinical-superiority claim.

The fixed intensity, Gaussian-noise, and blur scenarios are two-dimensional pixel diagnostics. They do not reproduce physical MRI acquisition failures, validate unsupported modalities, or prove that corner/background shortcuts have been removed. MCI scores cannot be reported as binary accuracy, early-AD detection, or progression prediction. No fitted calibration, abstention policy, prospective silent trial, or neuroradiologist adjudication is implemented by this comparison. Such components would require independent protocols and suitable new data before clinical-use claims.

The [evaluation protocol](../round2_clinical/evaluation_protocol.json), [clinical statistics](../round2_clinical/clinical_statistics.py), and independently SHA-pinned evaluation manifest define the intended analysis. AlzaSet remains outside this pilot while its patient mapping and archive counts are unresolved. No new clinical model inference was performed for this review.
